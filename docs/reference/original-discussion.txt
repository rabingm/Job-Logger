The key idea is to separate your content from your presentation. Your career is a bank of logged activities, and each resume is just a tailored view of that bank. This is what makes tailoring fast and sets you apart from VisualCV and Canva.
The core flow
Log: Capture a win in 30 seconds with one line, an optional metric, and a few skill tags. Weekly nudges keep the bank fresh, and AI can polish the wording.
Compose: A “version” is a selection and ordering of items from your bank, plus a headline and summary. Tailoring means toggling items on or off, dragging to reorder, and tweaking wording. Pasting a job ad could have AI suggest which items to surface, with you approving every change.
Share: Each version gets its own link (e.g. name.app/company-role) and a QR code. Add view tracking so you know when someone opened it.
UI principles (simpler than Canva)
Blocks, not a free canvas. Users never drag pixels around. Two or three strong templates handle layout.
Edit in place. You click text on the live resume and type. The side panel only holds visibility toggles and ordering.
“Duplicate for this job” in one tap. Versions must be nearly free to create.
Mobile-first. People apply from their phones, and QR sharing happens in person.
Interactive depth. Items expand to show detail, proof links, and projects, which a PDF can’t do.
Two honest cautions
Many applicant tracking systems still demand a file upload, so keep a quiet “export PDF” escape hatch until links become normal. Eliminating PDFs is the destination, not day one.
Give every link privacy controls: hidden contact details, expiry, and a revoke option.
MVP scope: Login, activity log, versions, 2–3 templates, link and QR, and view analytics. Skip the network for now. The resume tool is the single-player value that later seeds the talent network, where candidates opt in to being discoverable and employers search structured data instead of keyword-matching PDFs.

Try the eye toggles and the plus buttons, since both work. Here’s what each numbered part does:
Version bar and job ad strip. Every resume is a named version of your master profile. Pasting a job ad would rank your logged items by fit, and you’d approve each suggestion.
Edit in place. You tap any line on the resume itself to select it. A small toolbar appears for reword, shorten, and move. There’s no separate form view.
Hide, don’t delete. The eye icon removes an item from this version only. It stays in your master bank for other applications.
Suggestions from your log. This connects the activity log to tailoring. One tap on plus pulls a relevant past win into this version.
Bottom bar. Log is the quick-capture entry point, Items is the full bank, Style switches between the 2–3 templates, and Share opens the link and QR sheet.
The screen has no toolbars, rulers, or free-drag canvas. Everything you can do is selecting, toggling, reordering, or rewording.
